# LẮNG USER GUIDE (ENGLISH)

> **Lắng — the talking globe**: hear and touch NASA data, made for people who are blind or have low vision.
> Web version: **sense-app-two.vercel.app** · Team Lắng · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge #14 "The Earth Information Jukebox"
> Updated: 14 November 2026. New: **Earth Jukebox** (hear 5 NASA data visualizations, section 6); "past and present" uses one consistent data source; moon counts follow NASA's latest figures.
>
> This guide is for **users**, **companions** and **test facilitators**. When guiding a blind user, read the example phrases (in bold quotes) aloud.
> *Lắng* is Vietnamese for "listen closely". The app speaks Vietnamese and English; this guide uses **English mode**.

---

## 1. Three things to remember

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Give a command** | Just speak normally. The app is always listening; no buttons needed. A short **"ding"** means the command was heard. |
| **Interrupt the app** | Say **"stop"**, **"enough"** or **"be quiet"**. |
| **Get out, never get stuck** | Say **"go back"** or **"home screen"**. Or **draw a Z** on the screen with one finger, in under one second, or **swipe down with two fingers**. |
| **Talk over the app** | **Hold one finger still** anywhere on the screen for about half a second: the app stops, vibrates lightly, beeps and listens. Lift your finger when you have finished speaking. (Not on the touch-map screens, where holding a finger reads the place.) |

Not sure what to say? Say **"help"**. For one part, say **"help weather"**, **"help geography"** or **"help space"**. To hear NASA data images as sound, say **"Earth Jukebox"**.

To move between the parts quickly, **swipe left or right with two fingers**: weather → geography → space → Earth Jukebox.

---

## 2. Getting started

### 2.1. Switch to English

The app starts in Vietnamese. Say **"English"** (or **"tiếng Anh"**) to switch; say **"Vietnamese"** to switch back. The choice is remembered. You can also change it under the gear icon (settings).

### 2.2. Web version (computer or phone)

1. Open **Google Chrome** or **Microsoft Edge** and go to **sense-app-two.vercel.app**. Safari on iPhone may recognise speech less well.
2. When the browser asks for the **microphone**, choose **Allow**. A companion only needs to do this once.
3. The app greets you, introduces its parts, then turns on the microphone. Start speaking.
4. The web version has every part of the app, including the **Earth Jukebox**. The first time you open the Jukebox, the browser downloads about 2.4 MB of images and data; after that it opens instantly.
5. On the web, climate history and "past and present" need an internet connection. The **⌨️ Type a command** button replaces the voice when the computer has no microphone.

### 2.3. App version (Android, Windows)

1. Open the Lắng app. The first time, allow the **microphone** and, if you want to use "around me", **location**.
2. The app listens and speaks **without internet**, in Vietnamese and English (English speech recognition is built in; nothing is downloaded). Today's weather, the detailed touch map, the astronomy picture and the AI assistant still need a connection.

### 2.4. Tips for clear recognition

- Speak clearly, one sentence at a time. Wait for the app to finish, or say "stop", before asking again.
- Keep the speaker at a moderate volume. The app ignores its own voice in the microphone, but a very loud speaker in an echoing room can still cause mistakes.
- Hesitations and self-corrections are fine, for example: **"weather in Hue, no, Da Nang"**.
- Vietnamese place names work without accents: **"Hue"**, **"Da Nang"**, **"Ha Long"**.

## 3. Part 1 — Weather and climate

### 3.1. Weather now and forecasts

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"weather in Hanoi"** | Reads the current weather with travel advice; the *đàn bầu* zither and *t'rưng* bamboo xylophone play the numbers |
| **"will it rain this afternoon"**, **"will it rain tomorrow"** | Answers from the hourly forecast (48 hours) |
| **"is tomorrow good for a picnic in Da Lat"** | Forecast for that place with advice |
| **"10 day forecast"**, **"next 3 days"** | Daily forecast |
| **"any storms"**, **"storms around the world"** | Checks for dangerous weather and natural events |
| **"temperature in London"**, **"UV index in Sydney"**, **"air quality in Bangkok"** | Answers just that one value |
| **"play music"** | Hear the temperature played on the *đàn bầu* |

Any city in the world works for the current weather and forecast.

### 3.2. Weather history and disasters

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"weather history of Hue over the last 3 years"** | Replays each month as sound. The app says the last year NASA has published |
| **"disaster history in Quang Ngai"** | Replays storms, floods and landslides recorded there |
| **"Typhoon Yagi"** | Re-creates 15 days of Typhoon Yagi (September 2024) |
| **"Hue floods 2020"** | Re-creates 15 days of the Central Vietnam floods (October 2020) |
| **"climate of Hanoi"**, **"Hanoi past and present"** | Compares the 1980s with the last 10 years: temperature, days above 35, 37 and 39 °C, very heavy rain days, yearly rainfall, hottest year; then plays one *đàn bầu* note per year |
| **"July 2024 in Hue"**, **"2020 in Da Nang"** | NASA POWER data for that month or year compared with normal |
| **"global warming"** | The *đàn bầu* sweeps through 146 years of global temperature (NASA GISTEMP) |
| **"FIRMS fire data"** | Fire counts by month, played as crackling fire (currently illustrative seasonal figures; being replaced with FIRMS satellite counts) |

While a history is playing, **tap the screen** to pause and hear an explanation of that moment. Say **"go back"** to leave.

> **About "past and present":** monthly and yearly figures come from NASA POWER. The past-vs-present comparison (warming, hot days, yearly rain) comes from the **European ERA5 climate reanalysis**, which is one consistent series for all 45 years; NASA POWER's series for Vietnam has jumps that can lead to wrong conclusions. The app names this source aloud. **Offline**, the app only reads recent levels and **does not state a trend**.

## 4. Part 2 — Geography around you

### 4.1. Explore by touch (feel a real terrain map)

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"explore by touch in Da Lat"** | Opens a real terrain map about 2.4 × 2.4 km around Da Lat |
| **"explore by touch around me"** | Opens the map around where you stand (needs location) |
| **"touch terrain"** | Feel the terrain of the globe |

**North is at the top.** Place one finger on the screen and slide slowly:

- Going higher, the **bamboo flute** rises in pitch; going lower, it drops.
- On steep ground the phone **vibrates** more strongly. On the web, only Android phones can vibrate.
- Sound moves left or right with west and east.
- At each new spot, the app reads the **name, elevation and slope**.

River and street names may appear a few seconds after the map opens. Say **"exit"**, **"close the map"** or **"go back"** to leave.

### 4.2. Scan the terrain

**"scan the terrain of Da Lat"**: the app sweeps an area about 100 km around that place, north to south, then west to east, each pass under 8 seconds. The flute rises and falls with elevation, so you can hear mountains and valleys.

Also try: **"how high is Sa Pa"**, **"highest mountain in Vietnam"**, **"terrain from Hanoi to Sa Pa"**, **"along Vietnam"**.

### 4.3. What is around, where am I

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"what's around Hoan Kiem Lake"** | Places to visit near that spot |
| **"famous places"**, **"places to visit"** | Landmarks near the place you are viewing |
| **"where am I"** | Reads your current location from GPS |
| **"where is this"** | Reads the place shown on the globe |
| **"I live in Cau Giay"** | Saves your home for quick questions later |

Restaurants and hotels in Hanoi are **temporarily hidden**. If you ask about them, the app will say so.

## 5. Part 3 — Explore space

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"solar system"** | Opens the Solar System; every body has its own sound |
| **"tell me about Mars"** | NASA facts and stories about Mars (10 bodies: the Sun, 8 planets and the Moon) |
| **"fun fact about Saturn"**, **"another fun fact"** | Hear NASA fun facts |
| **"how many moons does Saturn have"**, **"how big is Jupiter compared to Earth"** | Answers from NASA data. Moon counts per NASA, August 2026: Saturn 293, Jupiter 115, Uranus 29, Neptune 16 |
| **"NASA sounds"**, **"play the Sputnik sound"** | Hear 10 real recordings: Sputnik, Apollo 11, Apollo 13, Saturn's radio waves… |
| **"picture of the day"** | Hear NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day explained (needs internet) |
| **"is the Earth flat"**, **"Voyager"**, **"James Webb"** | Checked answers that correct common misconceptions |

### 5.1. Games (in the space part)

1. Say **"play a game"**. The app lists 4 games: **Quiz**, **Who am I** (guess the planet from clues), **Guess by sound**, and **NASA sound quiz**.
2. Choose one: **"quiz"**, **"who am I"**, **"guess the sound"**, or **"game three"**.
3. Answer by voice. While playing:
   - **"repeat"**: hear the question again;
   - **"hint"**: get a hint;
   - **"skip"**: next question;
   - **"stop"**: end the game.
4. High scores are saved on the device.

## 6. Part 4 — Earth Jukebox (hear NASA data visualizations)

NASA publishes many **data images and videos** of Earth (Scientific Visualization Studio, Earth Information Center). The Earth Jukebox turns five of them into sound, with a spoken description of each image for people who cannot see it. It works **offline**.

### 6.1. Open and choose an image

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"Earth Jukebox"** | Lists the 5 images, then plays the first |
| **"Earth Jukebox list"** | Only reads the list |
| **"listen to night lights"** | City lights around the world, one beat per year, 2012 to 2023 |
| **"listen to lightning"** | Lightning seen from the International Space Station, one beat per month (2017–2023 average) |
| **"listen to CO2"** | Carbon dioxide near the ground, January to March 2020, one beat per day |
| **"listen to ocean temperature"** | Sea-surface temperature and currents, one beat per month; the main voice follows the **South China Sea (East Sea)** |
| **"listen to forest height"** | Forest canopy height measured by laser (NASA GEDI), swept west to east |

Each image starts with a **description** (what the picture looks like) and a **legend** (what each sound means), then plays.

### 6.2. Controls while listening

| Say | The app |
|---|---|
| **"pause"** / **"continue"** | Pause / resume |
| **"faster"** / **"slower"** | 5 speed levels |
| **"replay"**, **"play again"** | Start again from the beginning |
| **"year 2020"**, **"month 7"**, **"next"**, **"previous"** | Jump to that frame, read its numbers, play just that frame |
| **"describe"** | Describe the image and the current frame again |
| **"legend"** | What each sound means |
| **"explain"**, **"why"** | Why this instrument was chosen for this data |
| **"source"** | NASA source and SVS visualization ID |
| **"switch to lightning"**, **"listen to forest height"** | Change image |
| **"go back"**, **"exit"** | Leave the Jukebox |

After playing, the app **sums up in words**, for example: *"From 2012 to 2023 the global level rose about 17 percent. Vietnam and the East Sea rose about 44 percent."* Saying a different command (like "weather in Hue") leaves the Jukebox and runs that command.

### 6.3. Touch the image

Place a finger on the world map image (north at the top, west on the left) and slide:

- the instrument's pitch follows the value under your finger;
- rest for about half a second and the app reads the **region** (Vietnam, East Sea, Africa, Pacific Ocean…) and the **level**: very low, low, medium, high, very high. Where there is a unit it adds a number, for example "warm sea, about 29 °C" or "forest about 25 metres tall";
- crossing from one level to another gives a light vibration.

### 6.4. What the sounds mean

| Sound | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sustained instrument** (*đàn bầu* zither; flute for the forest image), higher or lower | The main value of that beat: brighter lights, more lightning, more CO₂, warmer sea, taller forest |
| **Percussion** (*t'rưng*, drum, crackle), denser or sparser | Area: the more places with high values, the denser the beats |
| Sound in the **left or right ear** | The strongest region is to the **west** or **east** |
| **Thunder, wind or waves** rising | This beat changed a lot from the previous one |
| **A small gong** | Vietnam and the East Sea changed noticeably |

## 7. Ask anything (AI assistant)

If there is no ready-made answer, the app asks an AI assistant and gives it the relevant real data. For example:

- **"what should I bring to Da Lat tomorrow"**
- **"why does Central Vietnam flood in October"**
- **"why is Venus hotter than Mercury"**

AI answers can take a few seconds and need the internet. For important numbers, ask again with a built-in command ("weather in…", "weather history of…") to hear the original data.

## 10. For companions

- **The row of buttons at the bottom** (My location, Hanoi, Weather history, 🎼 Earth Jukebox, Terrain scan, Explore by touch, Mars, NASA sounds…): tapping one is the same as saying that command.
- **Keyboard icon** at the top: opens **"Type a command"** to type instead of speaking. In games you can type answers here.
- **? icon**: help page. **Gear icon**: settings.
- The touch map, terrain scan and Earth Jukebox screens have an **"Exit"** button at the top left. In the Jukebox, a companion can place the user's hand on the map image to explore.
- If the user runs **TalkBack** (Android) or **VoiceOver** (iPhone), the screen reader may capture the touch gestures and the Z gesture. Pause the screen reader while using Lắng, or say "go back" instead of drawing the Z.

## 11. Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| The app hears nothing | Check the browser **allows the microphone** (padlock icon next to the address). Reload the page. Use Chrome or Edge |
| The app misunderstands | Say it again slowly and clearly, or correct it: **"not Hue, Da Nang"** |
| Still answering in Vietnamese | Say **"English"** once; the app switches and remembers |
| "The weather server is busy" | Wait a few seconds and ask again |
| Touch map has little detail, no river or street names | Weak or no internet: the app uses its built-in coarse elevation map. Reconnect and open it again |
| No vibration | On the web, only Android phones vibrate. iPhones and computers cannot vibrate through the browser |
| The app is reading something long | Say **"stop"** |
| Lost in the app | Say **"home screen"** or draw a Z |
| Jukebox has no images | The installed version is missing the `assets/eic` data. Install the latest version |
| The app crashed before | On the next start, the app turns off the part that failed and tells you. Other parts still work |

## 12. Limits to know

- NASA POWER climate data has a grid of about **50 km**, so it cannot show rain on a single street or village. Its long series for Vietnam has jumps, so the **past and present** comparison uses the ERA5 reanalysis (needs internet).
- **Offline**, touching terrain inside Vietnam uses a built-in grid of about **5.5 km** cells: you can hear mountains, plains, big rivers, lakes and cities, but not individual streets. Each cell is an average, so the cell containing Fansipan reads about 2,200 m, not 3,143 m.
- Numbers in the **Earth Jukebox** are **read from the colours of NASA's images**. They are good for hearing trends and comparing places, but they are not original measurements. Sea temperature read from an image, for example, may be 1–2 °C too high.
- Land cover (forest, fields, towns) is detailed to about **500 m**. River and street names come from OpenStreetMap.
- Weather history only goes up to the last year NASA has published. The app always says the final year with data.
- The AI assistant can make mistakes. Built-in commands are more reliable than free-form AI answers.
